Put your knowledge to work

Useful beyond the person who wrote it.

The explanation already exists. Help the next person, or their AI assistant, find it. These example workflows show what to explore in the technical beta.

Teams · Private LAN sharing

Make another team's expertise accessible.

Support needs an answer that engineering has already documented.

Example question“How do we recover a failed import?”

Engineering reviews its troubleshooting Wiki and grants access to verified colleagues on the local network. Support can search it and read the explanation. A connected assistant can retrieve the evidence when the owner permits that AI access.

Give colleagues a way to find answers without asking you to resend the same docs. Each owner chooses which wikis to share.

Private sharing currently uses authorized LAN devices. There is no SSO or managed private network between offices.

Students · Public discovery

Let your study notes help the next class.

A clear explanation can help classmates and students taking the same subject elsewhere.

Example question“Why does this database need normalization?”

Turn your own notes into a Wiki, review the concepts, and choose to publish it to the experimental public network. Other students can discover the topic and read your explanation with its sources. AI study apps can search it when public search is enabled.

Build on what other students have already worked through. Keep personal notes private and publish only material you have the right to share.

Public sharing is experimental. Publishers must be reachable through configured indexes; there is no supported always-on relay service. Readers can retain published content.

Developers · Project memory

Give the next AI session your project's context.

The README explains setup. Your project memory keeps the reasoning behind decisions.

Example question“Why do imports run in a background worker?”

Initialize a portable .airwiki Wiki in your repository. Authorize Codex, Claude Code, or Gemini CLI to capture confirmed decisions and consult them in later tasks. Review that knowledge as readable files alongside your code.

Spend less effort rebuilding context in every conversation. Keep conventions, architecture decisions, and reusable procedures available across compatible tools.

You control what enters Git. AirWiki never commits or pushes; a new clone needs local approval before an assistant can use its memory.

What “useful to AI” means

Give your assistant context it can look up.

Connect a compatible AI app through MCP, the connection protocol used by AI tools. It can retrieve relevant passages with source references from the knowledge it is allowed to access, as it works.

  • Knowledge with structure. Portable OKF pages organize concepts, relationships, and provenance.
  • Evidence on demand. Local search finds relevant passages so you can stop pasting the same background into every chat.
  • Access you choose. AI-app permissions, private sharing, and public publication have separate controls.

AirWiki prepares and searches knowledge locally. A connected cloud AI provider may process evidence its app receives. This is retrievable context, not model training or a measured claim of token savings.

An open foundation · OKF v0.2

Your knowledge, in a format you can inspect and take with you.

AirWiki uses Open Knowledge Format (OKF), an open specification introduced by Google Cloud for knowledge that people and AI agents can read. It gives your team docs, study notes, and project decisions a common structure, independent of a particular AI model or cloud provider.

  • Readable files. Markdown pages and structured YAML metadata let you inspect the knowledge in a text editor and review changes in Git.
  • Portable knowledge. Export an OKF Wiki and reuse its files with compatible tools, keeping concepts and their relationships together.
  • Traceable context. OKF v0.2 records provenance, verification, and freshness metadata so you can inspect where knowledge came from and how it was reviewed.

Private by default

You choose who can use your knowledge.

  • New wikis are private from LAN and the Internet.
  • Original files remain on the source device and AirWiki does not delete them.
  • Choose separately whether to share a Wiki, let an AI app read it, or let that app search public knowledge.
  • Only stable knowledge enters search or external disclosure.

Clear limits are part of the product

What AirWiki does not promise.

No stable desktop download today. The public technical beta is for evaluators and is never selected by the updater.

No cloud sync, accounts, SSO, or web/mobile apps. AirWiki is a local desktop application.

No automatic publishing or access grants. A local model can propose metadata, not make trust decisions.

No arbitrary remote editing. MCP mutation is limited to explicitly authorized personal or project memory wikis.

Questions before evaluation

FAQ

Can I use AirWiki as a stable product today?

No. AirWiki has no supported stable public release. The available technical pre-releases are manual test candidates only.

Do I need to upload my documents?

No. Preparation and retrieval run locally. Sharing exposes the published Wiki; original source files stay on their owner's device. Connected AI apps may receive permitted evidence, which a cloud AI provider may process.

Does using an AI app publish my knowledge?

No. AI-app access, LAN sharing, Internet exposure, and public-query search are separate decisions with their own controls.

Where can I ask for help or contribute?

Read the support guide, inspect open issues, or consult the evaluation guide.
